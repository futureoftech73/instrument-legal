# Privacy policy for Instrument

Last updated 12 November 2026.

## The short version

Instrument collects nothing. There is no account, no sign-in, no server and no
analytics. Everything you write stays in one file inside the app's own folder on
your device. Nobody else can read it, including the people who made the app.

## What the app stores, and where

Your record is a single SQLite database inside this app's container on your
device. It holds the sessions you type or paste, the days you log, the figures
read from Health, photos you name so the app can recognise a machine, and the
settings you choose.

That file is covered by iOS file protection, so it is encrypted while your
device is locked.

## Health data

The app asks to READ these kinds of Health data, and no others:

sleep, resting heart rate, heart rate variability, steps, workouts, active
energy, body mass, respiratory rate and VO2 max.

It asks so those figures are measured rather than typed in by you.

- The app requests no permission to WRITE to Health and cannot write a sample
  back into it.
- Health data is used only to work out, on your device, how recovered you are
  compared with your own earlier figures.
- Health data is never used for advertising or marketing, never shared with
  anyone, never sold, and never sent off your device.
- You can refuse Health access entirely and the app still works. Nothing breaks
  and nothing is withheld for refusing.
- You can withdraw access at any time in the Health app, under Sharing, then
  Apps.

## What leaves your device

Two things, and only one of them carries anything at all about you.

A purchase. When you buy the unlock or subscribe, the app talks to Apple's App
Store through StoreKit so Apple can take the payment and tell the app whether
you are entitled. That connection is between you and Apple, under Apple's own
privacy policy. The app never sees your payment details.

A request that Apple show its rating alert. Once, after a report has opened,
and never again. It carries nothing about you, it reads nothing back, and
whether the alert appears at all is Apple's decision, not the app's.

Apart from those two, the app makes no network connection of any kind. It has
no analytics, no crash reporting, no advertising identifier and no third-party
software development kit of any sort.

## What we collect

Nothing. We receive no data about you or your use of the app. We do not know
that you installed it. Apple may give the developer anonymous, aggregate sales
figures through App Store Connect, which say how many copies sold in which
country; those come from Apple and contain nothing identifying you.

## Your data is yours

- **Read it:** the database file is visible in the Files app, under Instrument.
  Copy it off whenever you like. It is an ordinary SQLite file and needs no
  special software to open.
- **Delete it:** delete the app and the file goes with it. There is nothing left
  on any server, because there is no server.
- **Correct it:** the log is append only by design, so a correction is recorded
  beside the original rather than replacing it. That is deliberate: it means the
  record of what you actually wrote at the time cannot be quietly rewritten.
- There is nothing to request from us and nobody to ask, because we hold no copy
  of your data.

## Children

The app is not directed at children and collects no data from anyone.

## Not medical advice

Instrument is a training log. What it suggests is not medical advice and not a
diagnosis. Ask a doctor before beginning to train, particularly if you are
starting out, coming back from an injury or illness, pregnant, or managing a
condition.

## Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the date at the top changes with it. Since the app
collects nothing, a change here would mean the app had started doing something
new, and that would arrive in an app update you choose to install.
